Tone Guide: High – Low | Low – High – Mid (Igbo)

Meaning: "Chika" (Chukwuka) means "God is supreme" in Igbo.

CHAPTER ONE • COMMUNITY MODE

The Fabric of Memory

On the morning of the celebration, the compound smelled of fried plantains, woodsmoke, and fresh starch. Udodi stood by the veranda watching her aunties unpack bolts of matching aṣọ-ẹbí carefully folded in brown paper.

[Yorùbá / Igbo Parallel]: Ní òwúrọ̀ ayẹyẹ náà, agbo ilé náà kún fún òórùn dòdò àti aṣọ-ẹbí tuntun tí wọ́n fi ṣe àṣà ìṣọ̀kan ẹbí.

"Do not stand there looking like an òyìnbó visitor," her mother laughed, tying her headwrap with practiced speed. "Bring the kola nuts and greet your elders properly—nnọ́ọ̀, welcome home."

[Igbo Parallel]: "Eguzola ebe ahụ dị ka onye ọbịa. Weta ọjị ka i kelee ndị okenye gị—nnọ́ọ̀, nnabata n'ụlọ."

Across the courtyard, someone tuned a radio to highlife music, and the rhythm carried over the red laterite wall into the warm afternoon air, binding every voice in the neighborhood into a single ubuntu chorus.

[Cultural Note]: Highlife guitars and talking rhythms echo across West African courtyards, grounding the narrative in communal belonging.

YORÙBÁ WORD

Aṣọ-ẹbí

/ah-SHAW eh-BEE/ • Literal: "Family Cloth"

A traditional Nigerian practice where family members, friends, and community wear matching fabrics at weddings, funerals, and festivals to show solidarity and shared identity.
